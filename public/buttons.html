Layout study · metal skin unchanged

Eight layouts.
One skin.

Same metal, same type, same gold ramp — only the composition inside the box changes. The gradient now sweeps rather than snaps: it is drawn at 220% width and its position is animated over 750ms, because CSS cannot transition a background-image itself. Hover slowly and watch the light travel across.

The question here is where the content sits inside the box — not what colour the box is.

Justified

01

Label flush left, mark flush right, air between them. The box stops being a container around a word and becomes a bar with two ends.

Stacked

02

A second line carries the thing the shopper actually wants to know. Two levels of information inside one control — the most retail of the set.

Split cell

03

A hairline divides the action from its mark. Reads like a stamped plate with two fields — and echoes the tag logic in the wordmark.

Price cell

04

The amount lives inside the button, in tabular figures. One glance answers both what this does and what it costs — strong on a product page, wrong in a grid.

Icon lead

05

A ringed mark opens the line, label follows, everything left-aligned. Calmer than centred — it reads as a row, not a stamp.

Wide minimal

06

76px tall, 64px of padding either side, a 10px label at .42em tracking. Almost all of it is empty space — the most restrained, and the most expensive-feeling if the page around it is quiet enough to carry it.

Arrow travel

07

At rest it is a centred label with nothing else. On hover the text steps left and a mark arrives in the space it left. The composition changes on interaction rather than the colour.

Bar

08

Full-width with a quantity field held on the right behind a hairline. This is the sticky mobile checkout bar — one control doing two jobs.
