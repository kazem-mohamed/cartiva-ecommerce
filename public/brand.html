The mark

Constructed, not drawn

A ring with a rectangular bite taken from its right side, producing a C, with a disc seated in the mouth — a letter and a container at once. Every dimension comes from the geometry, so it reduces cleanly to 16px. No shopping-cart glyph: every marketplace already owns one, and the tag is the object that actually spans every category here. Gold is never one hex

The system rule

A metal edge needs a shadow end, a body and a highlight, or it reads as a coloured line rather than metal. Five stops, always used as a ramp — this single rule is what separates the system from a gold-coloured border.

Two deliberate exceptions. Success stays a deep green — that mapping is a real-world convention and a redundancy for colour-blind users alongside icon and label; it never appears in the logo, nav, footer, or any CTA. And the darkest ground is #08070A, not pure black, because pure black smears on OLED panels during scroll.

Archivo, two widths

Width is the contrast

Display runs expanded, body runs normal. The contrast comes from width rather than a second face — which keeps the system cohesive and gives it the signage character the tag idea wants. Archivo descends from grotesques built for price boards and wayfinding, and its tabular figures matter on a storefront full of prices.

Arrow Travel

The primary action

At rest it is a centred label and nothing else. On hover the label steps left and a mark arrives in the space it vacated — the composition changes, not the colour. Underneath, the gold sweeps rather than snaps: the ramp is drawn at 220% width and its position is animated over 750ms, because CSS cannot transition a background-image. Hover slowly. Click to load.

Gold metal edge. Add to cart, buy, submit. One per screen.

Primary · light twin

The same construction on paper, using the deep end of the ramp so it holds 4.68:1. This is what makes the system usable on category pages.

Disabled

28% opacity, no pointer, no sweep. Never a control that looks live and does nothing.

Quiet pill

Not a CTA. Filters and category chips only — full radius deliberately breaks the 1px rule so it can never read as a primary action.

The product card

Five layers, one gesture

The card is where the brand does most of its work, so its motion is authored rather than inherited. Hovering runs five things at once on a single 450–850ms curve: the card lifts, its edge warms to gold, the product scales inside a fixed frame, a gold hairline draws across the base, and the action bar rises. Everything moves on transform and opacity only — nothing reflows, so a grid of forty cards stays on the compositor thread.

Contained zoomThe product scales 1.06 inside a fixed 4:5 frame with overflow hidden. The card's own footprint never changes, so neighbours never shift.
Rising actionThe add-to-cart bar translates up from the frame's base, and its gold sweeps on its own hover — the same 220% mechanic as Arrow Travel.
Reachable on touchAll hover behaviour sits inside @media (hover:hover). On a phone the action bar and quick controls are permanently visible — a hover-only control is unreachable there.

Foundations

Spacing · radius · elevation

Spacing

  • 4 · 8 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 72 · 112
  • More space above a heading than below it, always.

Radius

  • 1px buttons, inputs
  • 2px cards, panels
  • 3px sheets, modals
  • full filter pills only

Elevation

  • On dark — a hairline plus a tone step. Shadows do not read on near-black.
  • On light — real offset plus soft blur.
  • Never a zero-offset coloured halo.

Imagery

  • The product, on a plain or softly defocused ground. Objects over lifestyle.
  • Even neutral light — no golden-hour cast, it re-codes the brand as food.
  • Never produce, stalls, baskets or crates.

The product silhouettes on the cards above are placeholder line art, not photography — this build has no owned product images, and the honesty rules below forbid borrowing any.

Honesty rules

A portfolio build on a public demo API. No real customers, revenue, ratings or testimonials — so it claims none. Violations here are brand violations, not content bugs.

  • No fabricated social proof. The pre-Cartiva build shipped "50K+ Users", a "4.9 Rating", and a testimonial from a person who does not exist. None of it returns.
  • No dead controls. If it renders, it does something. The Google and Facebook sign-in buttons were removed rather than faked.
  • No unowned assets. The old login hero was hot-linked from a design tool's temp bucket with the generator prompt still sitting in its alt text.
