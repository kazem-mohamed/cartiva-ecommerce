01

Foundation

Cartiva presents everyday things the way a gallery presents objects after hours: a dark room, one light, nothing competing. The product is always the brightest thing on the screen.

Positioning

A general marketplace — electronics and fashion today — whose premium lives in the presentation, not the price tag. For people who want to see exactly what they are buying and decide without noise.

Premium, honestly

Cartiva does not claim luxury goods. It claims care: every product gets the same light, the same space and the same accurate price — from a EGP 149 shawl to a EGP 42,960 television.

Visual philosophy

Darkness is the canvas; light is the content. One light source — cobalt — marks the one thing to do. Elevation comes from value, never shadow. Pills for what you touch, 12px corners for what you look at.

Customer perception

Walking into a well-kept gallery after hours: nothing shouting, everything findable, the object in front of you perfectly clear — and the way out to the till obvious.

Colour

A near-monochrome room with one light in it. Every colour has a single job; light-mode values are derived from the same nine brand colours, never invented.

The rules

  1. One cobalt per view. It marks the single thing to do next — add to bag, checkout, place order. Never decoration, never text.
  2. Sale is monochrome. 27 of 56 products are discounted; red on half the grid would read as a clearance bin. Struck price in --text-3, a quiet chip for the percentage.
  3. One red, errors only. Form validation and destructive actions — always with an icon and a sentence, never colour alone.
  4. No shadows on dark. Separation is a step in value: canvas → surface → raised.

  • Nothing heavier than 530. Nothing lighter than 360.
  • Sentence case everywhere. No all-caps paragraphs.
05

Imagery

Two kinds of image, never mixed up. Product photos come from the catalogue, untouched, on a lit plate. Editorial images are one bright studio and one soft daylight from the upper left — the logo's own light — with people using things the way they're meant to be used.

The lit plate

Every product sits on a plate one step brighter than the canvas. 47 of 56 catalogue photos are shot on white; multiplied onto the plate, the white becomes the plate, so every photo looks like it came from the same studio. The nine with a grey studio backdrop keep it — the plate never paints over a photograph.

The editorial look

  • Bright, airy studio on pale cool-grey seamless paper.
  • One large soft daylight source from the upper left.
  • Egyptian and Mediterranean models in neutral wardrobes; devices shown in use.
  • At most one muted accent colour per image. Never warm, golden or orange.
  • Generic, unbranded objects only — never a stand-in for a catalogue product. No text, no logos.

07

Motion

Motion is light arriving, not objects bouncing. Nothing overshoots. Every movement answers something the shopper did, and all of it stops when reduced motion is on.

Try it

Transforms and opacity only. prefers-reduced-motion swaps every movement for a 120ms fade.

Lights on

Images and plates rise out of black, then the rim sweeps in from the upper left. Once per surface, never on scroll-back.

Pill morph

On a product page the floating mobile pill becomes the add-to-bag bar. One object, two jobs.

10

Honesty

This is a portfolio store on a public demo API. It claims only what is true. A violation here is a brand violation, not a content bug.

No fabricated proof

No invented ratings, counts, testimonials or press logos. Ratings and reviews shown are the ones the store actually has.

No false newness

All 56 products were added in March 2023, so nothing is labelled "new". Rankings use real fields — sold, discount, rating.

No dead controls

If it renders, it works. No size selector without sizes, no free-shipping bar without a shipping rule.

No stand-in products

Editorial images show generic, unbranded objects — never a fake version of something in the catalogue.
